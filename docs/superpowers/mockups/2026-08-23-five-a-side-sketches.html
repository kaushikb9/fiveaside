Five-a-Side

Turning a personal FPL planner into something five friends actually open. My read on the brain dump, the one structural idea I think everything should hang off, and rough wireframes for each surface — sketches, not builds.

Annotate this. Tap ✎ Comment bottom-right, then any block. Notes live in this browser; Export for Claude hands you a markdown file to drop back into a session. Nothing here is built — it's all disposable until you say otherwise.
01

What changed, in one line

It stops being my planner that friends can look at and becomes a five-handed product with a shared spine. That's not a redesign — it's a different application with the same data plumbing, and the pieces we've built (facts CLI, validator, brain, live proxy) all survive. What has to change is the shape of the data, because five people cannot each get a bespoke page written from scratch every day.

The good news: your brain dump already contains the solution, at the bottom, described as a nice-to-have. The player DB isn't a fifth tab. It's the thing the other four are made of.

02

The structural idea: the player file is the spine

Right now every section writes its own prose about players. The watchlist says something about Thiago, a signal says something about Thiago, the call mentions Thiago. Three writes, three chances to contradict ourselves, and it multiplies by five people.

Invert it. There is one record per player carrying both the standard numbers and — the part nobody else does — our verdict in plain English. Every surface becomes a filtered view of those records. Write once, render five ways.

The five words, and nothing else. Essential · Solid · Watch · Cooling · Avoid. A fixed vocabulary is what makes it a database instead of an opinion column — you can sort by it, filter by it, and notice when it changes. The one sentence carries the nuance; the word carries the sort.

On your "useless numbers" complaint — I'd go further than dropping xG. The rule I'd write into the prompt: a number may only appear if it's the evidence for a sentence we already wrote. Never a stat table for its own sake. "Started every game since the derby, 90 minutes in all five" earns its place; "0.42 xGI/90" does not, unless it's the reason for the verdict.

Why the trigger line matters more than it looks. It's the same falsifiability trick as the wagers, one level down. A verdict with a written trigger can be settled later — "we said watch him if he starts twice; he did; we were late." That's how the ledger gets fed automatically instead of by hand.

Dropping "the call" is right, and here's the deeper reason. A single prescriptive call only works when there's one reader to be accountable to. With five, the same sentence becomes either bossy or generic. Verdicts move down to the player file (where they're shared and sortable) and up to the weekly read (where they're personal and honest). The middle layer — "do this transfer" — is the bit that stops earning its keep.
Question buried in your dump: are wagers, ledger and doctrine per person or house-wide? I'd make doctrine house-wide (it's what the machine has learned about football), and wagers + ledger per person (they're your bets). Otherwise everyone inherits my Palmer wager, which is nonsense.

08

The roast, and how it stays funny

This is the highest-risk, highest-reward thing in the dump. Automated banter fails in two directions: cringe (a bot doing "bantz") or genuinely unkind. Rules I'd write into the prompt:

  • Only ever about a decision. Captaincy, a transfer, a bench call, a rank move. Never about a person, their club's form, or anything outside the game.
  • A fact must be attached. "Benched Hume for Diop and Hume returned 12" is funny because it's true. Freestanding insults aren't jokes.
  • Rotate, and never twice running. Whoever had the worst week has probably had enough; the machine should notice when someone's been the punchline two weeks straight and go elsewhere.
  • Roast the machine too. It told you to leave out every Arsenal player the week Arsenal hauled. Self-deprecation is what buys the licence to aim at anyone else.
  • Weekly, after the gameweek settles. Daily banter is forced by definition. Post-GW banter has material.
Worth settling before anything ships: this puts four named friends, their teams, and jokes at their expense on a public URL. Their FPL data is already public via the API, so it's not a data question — it's a social one. Options: first names only · an unguessable URL · a soft gate · or simply ask them (and let them see it, which is half the fun). My recommendation: tell them it exists and let it be public to the group. A product built to be shared works better when the people in it know.

How it gets built without the brain melting

Five people's worth of daily prose is the thing that would kill this. The spine solves it: the expensive writing is shared, and the per-person layer is thin.

Per-person writing is roughly a hundred words each, once a week. Everything else the five views need is a filter over shared records.

Order I'd build in

  • 1 · The player file. Nothing else can be built properly until this exists, and it immediately improves the page we already have.
  • 2 · Five people. Person selector, five desks, five pitches, the compressed table. Mostly plumbing we've done once already.
  • 3 · The Gaffers room proper. Weekly reads, wagers and ledger per person, then the roast on top once there's material to roast.
  • 4 · Home rebuild. The filtered news page — biggest editorial change, and it can run on the current digest brain while we get there.
  • 5 · The Scout as a browsable tab. Last, because by then the file is rich enough to be worth browsing.
