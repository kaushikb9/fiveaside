About

Following football shouldn't feel like work

Loving the beautiful game now comes with a tax. Keeping up means drowning in reddit threads about a referee you'll never think about again, or wading through football twitter, where every match is a war and every teenager a failure by half-time. The game is beautiful; the feeds about it mostly aren't.

Touchline is the opposite machine. One calm page each morning: what happened, what's coming, who's worth watching, one thing worth actually reading — and then it ends. There is nothing to pull down, nothing to refresh, nobody yelling. The absence of more is the feature.

Convictions baked in

  • The pulse, not the flood. Enough to stay on top of the game and hold your own at the pub — scores, the table, the week's real stories, the kid coming through at the academy. Never an overdose. If it doesn't help you love the game more, it doesn't make the page.
  • Fun is the point. Football is joy, drama, and occasionally heartbreak by the 94th minute. The digest is written like a friend catching you up over coffee, not a wire service — opinionated picks, honest quiet days, the odd wound reopened gently.
  • Local-first, yours-first. The editor is an AI brain that runs on my machine, with my club and my preferences, every morning with coffee. A static page and one JSON file are the whole product. Fork it, put your club in the config, run your own — nothing phones home.
  • Facts from data, color from discourse. Scores, tables and fixtures come from real match data; the mood comes from what fans are actually talking about. The brain is never allowed to invent a scoreline or a transfer fee — a validator checks its homework before anything ships.
  • Built to learn. Sibling of antifeed, and the same experiment underneath: what happens when you point an AI agent at a daily editorial job and let a human only steer taste? Building the machine is half the fun of owning it.
