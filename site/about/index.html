Following football shouldn't feel like work

Loving the beautiful game now comes with a tax. Keeping up means drowning in reddit threads about a referee you'll never think about again, or wading through football twitter, where every match is a war and every teenager a failure by half-time. The game is beautiful; the feeds about it mostly aren't.

Five-a-Side is the opposite machine. Three calm rooms, and then it ends. There is nothing to pull down, nothing to refresh, nobody yelling. The absence of more is the feature.

Three rooms, one rule each

touchline
What happened. The league, and identical for everyone who opens it. No squads, no personal state. If a thing here depends on who is reading, it belongs next door.
the gaffers
What we did about it. Five squads, five weekly reads, one mini-league, and a roast that is only ever about a decision someone actually made.
the locker room
What we know. Every player we hold evidence on, the injury room, and the fixture runs. The other two rooms link into this one — any player's name, anywhere, opens their file.

Convictions baked in

The pulse, not the flood
Enough to stay on top of the game and hold your own at the pub — scores, the table, the week's real stories. Never an overdose. If it doesn't help you love the game more, it doesn't make the page.
Facts from data, judgment marked as judgment
Prices, points, squads, chips and fixtures are copied from the official API and never pass through the editor — they cost six figures of tokens to retype and invite transcription errors. What the editor writes is opinion, and the page says so.
A verdict is a hypothesis with a trigger
Four words — nailed, solid, watch, sack — each with one line of why and, written before the fact, what would change our mind. That last part is the whole discipline: it makes a retro possible instead of a debate.
The long game
One weekend of upsets and stoppage-time luck is not evidence. Early in a season the page says so out loud, and retires the warning once there is enough football to argue from.
Nicknames only
The five are known by nickname here and nowhere else. The API hands back real names; they are deleted where the data is built, with a test and a validator to keep them deleted.
Built to learn
Sibling of antifeed, and the same experiment underneath: point an AI agent at an editorial job and let a human steer only taste. Building the machine is half the fun of owning it.
